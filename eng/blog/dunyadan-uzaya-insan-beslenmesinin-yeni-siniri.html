THE NEW FRONTIER OF NUTRITION

Nutrition in Space

Microgravity, radiation, and prolonged isolation in the space environment can cause various physiological changes in the human body. These changes can have a particular impact on the muscular and bone system, energy metabolism, and fluid balance. Therefore, adequate and balanced nutrition plays an important role in maintaining the health and physical performance of astronauts.

The Importance of Nutrients in Space Missions

Proteins play an important role in the protection and regeneration of muscle tissue. Since the decrease in mechanical load applied to the muscles due to microgravity can cause muscle loss, adequate protein intake and resistance exercise are evaluated together. Calcium and vitamin D are important for bone metabolism. Since bone mineral loss can be seen during long-term space flights, it is important to get enough of these nutrients.

Carbohydrates and fats are the main sources of energy. Not getting enough energy can lead to a decrease in body weight and lean body mass. Vitamins and minerals are necessary for energy metabolism, immune system, blood production and the continuity of cellular functions.

Space Foods

Space foods must be safe to store for a long time, preserve their nutritional value as much as possible and be low in weight. It is also important that food is easy to prepare and accepted by astronauts. Drying, heat treatment and special packaging methods are used to increase the shelf life of space foods.

On long-term missions to the Moon and Mars, it can be difficult to depend solely on food transported from Earth. For this reason, research is being done on growing plants in space, producing fresh food, and sustainable food systems.

Dietetics and Space Exploration

Dietitians can contribute to determining the energy and nutrient requirements of astronauts, monitoring food consumption and creating personalized nutrition plans. In the future, areas such as personalized nutrition, gut microbiota, artificial intelligence-supported nutrient tracking and food production in space are expected to develop.

Space nutrition is a multidisciplinary field of research where dietetics combines with physiology, food technology, and space medicine. Developing safe, adequate, and sustainable nutrition systems is crucial for people to stay healthy for extended periods in distant destinations such as the Moon and Mars.
