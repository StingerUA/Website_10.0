Exploring the Universe with a Wide Angle: Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope

Muzaffer Enes İpçi · Guest Writer · September 04, 2026 · 6 min read

Grace Roman Space Telescope

The stars and galaxies we see when we look at the sky make up only a small part of the universe. We need a much wider field of observation to understand how the universe formed, how it expands and how it changes over billions of years. The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope stands out as one of the next generation space telescopes that will allow astronomers to look at the universe from a broader perspective.

Who is Nancy Grace Roman?

The telescope is named after Nancy Grace Roman, one of NASA's pioneering scientists in astronomy. As one of NASA's first chief astronomers, Roman played a significant role in the development of space telescopes.

Roman, who advocated the importance of making astronomical observations, especially from space, also made important contributions to the development of the Hubble Space Telescope. For this reason, she is often referred to as "Hubble's mother".

What is the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope?

The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope is a large-area space observatory developed by NASA. Its main purpose is to study the universe in much larger regions and collect data on the properties of billions of cosmic objects.

One of the most notable features of Roman is its wide field of view. While Hubble can obtain very detailed images, Roman is designed to be able to spread similarly sharp observations over a much larger region of the sky.

This feature will be a great advantage for astronomers. Because understanding the universe is possible not only by studying individual stars or galaxies, but also by seeing how these objects are distributed with each other and how they change over time.

How is it different from Hubble and James Webb?

The Hubble Space Telescope has been obtaining the most impressive images of the universe in decades. The James Webb Space Telescope, on the other hand, helps us look at much earlier periods of the universe, especially thanks to its infrared observations.

The novel offers a different approach: wide-field observation.

The telescope will be able to scan large parts of the sky and examine many galaxies and stars in the same survey. Thus, astronomers will be able to investigate not only individual cosmic objects, but also the large-scale structure of the universe.

In short, Hubble will play an important role in creating large cosmic maps, while Hubble will excel in detailed images, James Webb in deep and infrared observations.

Investigating the Mystery of Dark Energy

One of Roman's most important scientific goals is to learn more about dark energy.

We know that the universe is expanding. However, observations show that this expansion is accelerating over time. One of the key concepts used to explain the mechanism behind this acceleration is dark energy.

But what dark energy really is is still a big mystery.

The novel will study millions of galaxies, investigating the distribution of galaxies in the universe and how they change over time. These observations can help us understand the expansion history of the universe more precisely.

Mapping Dark Matter Without Seeing It

Another great mystery of the universe is dark matter.

Dark matter cannot be seen directly because it does not emit light. However, due to its mass, it creates gravitational effects on the surrounding matter and light.

By studying how light bends due to gravity, the novel will be able to gather information about the distribution of dark matter in the universe.

Thanks to this method, astronomers will be able to follow the traces of a structure in the universe that they cannot see directly. Thus, a more detailed map of the invisible part of the universe can be created.

A New Era in the Hunt for Exoplanets

One of the topics Roman will explore is exoplanets, that is, planets outside the Solar System.

Thousands of exoplanets have been discovered to date. However, while the methods used are more successful in finding some types of planets, others can be very difficult to detect.

The novel will investigate planets with different properties, especially using the gravitational microlensing method.

In this method, the gravity of a star can temporarily magnify the light from another star behind it. If a planet is located around the foreground star, small changes in light may occur during this event. By analyzing these changes, astronomers can detect the presence of the planet.

This method could help find some planets that are very far away from their star or that are difficult to discover by other methods.

More Detailed Map of the Milky Way

The novel will not only look at distant galaxies. It will also examine our own galaxy, the Milky Way, in detail.

There are hundreds of billions of stars in the Milky Way. Studying the positions, properties and movements of these stars can help us understand the past of our galaxy.

Thanks to Roman's observations, new information can be obtained about how the Milky Way formed, what changes it has undergone over time, and how different collections of stars are distributed throughout our galaxy.

Millions of Galaxies in the Same Research

One of the most important consequences of Roman's wide field of view will be the emergence of a huge amount of astronomical data.

The telescope will be able to study the properties of millions of galaxies by scanning large regions of the sky. Such large datasets will allow astronomers to statistically probe the universe.

For example, by comparing galaxies of different ages, it can be investigated how galaxies have evolved over time. At the same time, by examining the distribution of galaxies, more detailed models can be created about the large-scale structure of the universe.

Maybe He Will Make Discoveries We Didn't Expect

One of the most exciting things about space telescopes is that they don't just find what we're looking for.

In the past, many astronomical discoveries have emerged from results that scientists did not predict. Roman's extensive scans of the sky could also lead to the discovery of previously unnoticed star systems, galaxies, or other cosmic phenomena.

Perhaps the telescope's most important discoveries will be related to topics that we don't even know what question we should ask today.

Looking at the Universe Through a Larger Window

The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope is not designed to replace Hubble or James Webb, but to complement their work.

Thanks to its wide field of view, it will be able to study much larger parts of the universe. It will conduct research in many different areas, from dark energy and dark matter to exoplanets, from the Milky Way to billions of distant galaxies.

The data Roman will provide can play an important role in our understanding of the past and future of the universe.

Perhaps the most important thing this telescope will show us is to remind us how small a fraction of what we know about the universe is. Because when we look at the sky, what we see is not only the image of the universe; it is also the door to billions of secrets that we have not yet discovered.
