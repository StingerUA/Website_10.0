We're Turning Space into a Dump: Are Future Space Flights in Danger?

Muzaffer Enes İpçi · Guest Writer · August 09, 2026 · 3 min read

We're Turning Space into a Dump: Are Future Space Flights in Danger? When we look up at the sky, we imagine space as an infinite and completely empty area. However, the orbits around the Earth are not as empty as we think. As a result of the space missions that humanity has been carrying out for decades, thousands of satellites, rocket parts, and debris of various sizes are moving around the Earth. Some of these objects are no longer in use, but they continue to exist in space. What is Space Debris? Space debris refers to the general term for human-made objects in Earth's orbit that no longer serve any purpose. Examples include satellites that have completed their lifespan, upper stages of rockets, parts left over from missions, and small pieces formed as a result of collisions. The main reason these pieces are hazardous is their speed. A piece of space debris in orbit can move at kilometers per second. Therefore, even a piece a few centimeters in size can cause significant damage if it collides with a working satellite or spacecraft.

The Real Danger: Kessler Syndrome When it comes to space pollution, one of the most notable concepts is Kessler Syndrome. Proposed by NASA scientists Donald J. Kessler and Burton G. Cour-Palais in 1978, this scenario suggests that if the density of objects in Earth's orbit reaches a certain level, collisions can increase in a chain reaction. For example, let's say two large pieces of space debris collide. This collision can result in the formation of hundreds or thousands of new pieces. Some of these pieces can collide with other satellites, creating even more debris. Thus, a single collision can trigger a chain of collisions over time. In the worst-case scenario of Kessler Syndrome, the density of space debris in certain orbits can become so high that these regions become extremely risky for new space missions.

How Does This Problem Affect the Earth? Space debris is not just a problem that astronauts may encounter. Many technologies in our daily lives rely on satellites. We use satellites for GPS and navigation systems, communication satellites, weather forecasts, television broadcasts, and observing the Earth. Therefore, losing the safety of the orbits can affect the future of systems that rely on space technology. Additionally, to ensure the safe execution of future missions to the Moon, Mars, and beyond, it is necessary to keep the orbits around the Earth under control.

Is it Possible to Clean Up Space? The solution to this problem is not just about not sending new satellites. Because a significant portion of the existing space debris is still in orbit. Scientists and space agencies are developing various technologies to track these objects, predict risky approaches, and safely remove some large pieces from orbit. In the future, it is planned to clean up some large pieces of space debris using robotic arms, net systems, or other capture technologies. At the same time, safer designs are being developed for new satellites so that they do not remain in orbit when their missions are completed.

The Future of Space Starts Today Humanity is sending more and more satellites and spacecraft into space every day. This situation creates great opportunities in many areas, from communication to science. However, the increasing use of space also brings new responsibilities. We need to think of space not just as an area to be explored, but also as an environment that needs to be protected. Because if we want to go to Mars, establish a base on the Moon, or explore other planets in the future, we first need to keep the path around the Earth safe. The fact that space is infinite does not mean that our trash is insignificant.
