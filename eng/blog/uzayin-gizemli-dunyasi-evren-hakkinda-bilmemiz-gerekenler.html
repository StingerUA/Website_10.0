The Mysterious World of Space: What We Need to Know About the Universe

Muzaffer Enes İpçi · Guest Author · August 28, 2026 · 8 min read

Throughout history, humanity has looked up at the sky and tried to understand its place in the universe. From the few thousand stars visible to the naked eye to advanced space telescopes capable of studying billions of galaxies, this journey has dramatically transformed our knowledge of the universe.

Today, we know a great deal about how planets form, how stars are born and die, and how black holes behave. Yet the universe still contains many unanswered questions.

So how large is space? Could life exist on other planets? How do black holes form? Could humanity live on other planets in the future?

In this article, we explore some of the most remarkable features of space and the major questions scientists are working to answer.

What Is Space?

Space is the vast environment beyond Earth's atmosphere that contains countless cosmic structures such as planets, stars, galaxies, asteroids, comets, and clouds of gas and dust.

Space is not completely empty. It contains particles, gases, electromagnetic radiation, and various forms of cosmic matter at extremely low densities.

One of the most striking characteristics of space is its enormous scale. The kilometers we use in everyday life are inadequate for expressing cosmic distances. For this reason, astronomers use units such as the light-year to describe much larger distances.

What Is a Light-Year?

A light-year is not a unit of time but a unit of distance. It is the distance light travels through a vacuum in one year.

Light travels at approximately 300,000 kilometers per second. Even at that speed, it takes about 8 minutes and 20 seconds for sunlight to reach Earth.

Light from more distant stars can take years, thousands of years, or even millions of years to reach Earth. This means that in astronomy, looking at a distant celestial object also means looking into its past.

For example, when we observe a star 1,000 light-years away, we are seeing light that left that star about 1,000 years ago.

Where Is Earth in the Universe?

Earth is the only planet in the Solar System known to host life. The Solar System itself is located within the Milky Way Galaxy.

The Milky Way is thought to contain hundreds of billions of stars. The Sun is only one of them.

Moreover, the Milky Way is just one of countless galaxies in the observable universe. At this scale, it becomes easier to understand how small Earth's physical place in the universe really is.

Even so, as far as we know, Earth is the only place where life has emerged.

Exoplanets: Worlds Beyond the Solar System

Planets located outside the Solar System are called exoplanets.

Since the first exoplanet discoveries, scientists have detected thousands of planets. Their properties vary greatly.

Some are similar in size to Earth, while others are gas giants much larger than Jupiter. Some orbit so close to their stars that conditions on their surfaces may be extremely hot.

Scientists are especially interested in planets located within their stars' habitable zones.

The habitable zone is the region around a star where temperatures may allow liquid water to exist on a planet's surface.

However, being located in the habitable zone does not mean that a planet definitely has life.

Is There Life in Space?

One of the biggest questions in space research is whether life exists beyond Earth.

So far, there is no confirmed evidence of life outside Earth. Nevertheless, scientists are studying many celestial bodies that may have conditions suitable for life.

Mars is one of the most important targets of this research. Strong evidence indicates that liquid water existed on the Martian surface in the past.

Jupiter's moon Europa and Saturn's moon Enceladus also attract significant scientific attention. There is strong evidence that oceans of liquid water may exist beneath their icy surfaces.

The main goal of this research is not only to find life, but also to understand the conditions under which life can emerge in the universe.

How Do Black Holes Form?

Black holes are cosmic structures where gravity is extraordinarily strong.

Inside a certain boundary around a black hole, not even light can escape. This boundary is called the event horizon.

Some black holes can form through processes that occur at the end of the lives of very massive stars.

In addition, supermassive black holes with masses millions or billions of times greater than that of the Sun are found at the centers of galaxies.

Black holes cannot be seen directly. However, we can detect their presence by observing their effects on nearby matter and light.

How Large Is the Universe?

We do not know exactly how large the entire universe is.

The region that scientists can observe is called the observable universe. Its diameter is estimated to be about 93 billion light-years.

An important point is that although the universe is only about 13.8 billion years old, the diameter of the observable universe is much greater than 13.8 billion light-years.

The reason is the expansion of the universe. While light travels toward us, space itself continues to expand.

The Universe Is Expanding

Observations made in the 1920s showed that distant galaxies are generally moving away from one another. These observations became important evidence that the universe is expanding.

Today, the accepted view is that the universe began expanding from a very hot and dense state about 13.8 billion years ago.

The model used to describe this process is called the Big Bang model.

The Big Bang should not be imagined as an ordinary explosion taking place somewhere in space. What expanded was space itself.

Dark Matter and Dark Energy

A significant part of what we know about the universe concerns things that we cannot actually see directly.

Dark matter does not interact directly with light, so it cannot be observed directly with telescopes. However, there is strong evidence for its existence from the motions of galaxies and its gravitational effects.

Dark energy is a concept associated with the accelerating expansion of the universe, and its nature is still not fully understood.

These two subjects are among the largest areas of research in modern cosmology.

Why Are Space Telescopes Important?

Earth's atmosphere blocks or alters some forms of electromagnetic radiation coming from space. For this reason, making certain observations from space provides major advantages.

Space telescopes allow us to obtain important information about star formation, the evolution of galaxies, the atmospheres of exoplanets, and the early stages of the universe.

Advanced infrared observations in particular help us study distant and ancient cosmic structures that were previously difficult to see.

Humanity's Future in Space

Space exploration is not limited to scientific discovery.

Future goals include carrying out longer-duration crewed missions on the Moon, studying Mars in greater detail, and developing new space technologies.

However, reaching other star systems remains an enormous challenge with today's technology.

For example, Alpha Centauri, one of the star systems closest to the Sun, is about 4.37 light-years away. With spacecraft used today, traveling this distance would take an extremely long time.

For this reason, faster propulsion systems, advanced robotic technologies, and long-duration crewed missions may play important roles in the future of space exploration.

There Is Still a Great Deal We Do Not Know About Space

Although scientists have solved many mysteries of space, numerous questions are still waiting to be answered.

  • How large is the entire universe?
  • What is the true nature of dark matter?
  • Why is dark energy accelerating the expansion of the universe?
  • Is there life beyond Earth?
  • How did the first galaxies form?
  • How did supermassive black holes emerge?

The answers to these questions could change not only astronomy but also our overall understanding of the universe.

Conclusion

Space is one of the largest scientific frontiers that humanity continues to explore.

Every new telescope, spacecraft, and research method reveals new information about the universe that we did not know before.

Today, we understand Earth's place in the universe much better than we did in the past. Yet what we know is still very limited compared with what remains to be discovered.

Perhaps a future discovery will change our ideas about life beyond Earth or reshape our current understanding of how the universe formed.

Understanding space is not only about studying distant planets and stars. It is also an attempt to understand the universe we live in and our own existence within it.
