HAS ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE SOLVED ONE OF THE MOST DIFFICULT QUESTIONS IN MATHEMATICS?

İdris Albayrak · Space Entrepreneur, Futurist, and Educator · September 11, 2026 · 6 min read

ONE OF THE DIFFICULT QUESTIONS

DID HE SOLVE IT?

The question that humanity has been seeking an answer to for nearly 90 years is now in front of artificial intelligence. Imagine asking an artificial intelligence a mathematical question. "How much is 2 + 2?" Of course, he can easily answer this. But what if you asked a question that the world's most successful mathematicians have been working on for nearly 90 years and is considered one of the biggest problems in mathematics? This is exactly what happened in such a development. OpenAI announced that its artificial intelligence system has produced the solution to the Navier-Stokes problem. This problem is among the famous Millennium Problems of mathematics.

But to understand what this problem is, let's first ask a very simple question:

How does water move?

Why does a glass of water move? Think of water flowing from a tap. Sometimes water flows smoothly. Sometimes vortices form. When you blow into the air, the air moves. The air passing over the wing of an airplane behaves in different ways. Giant currents form in the oceans. Winds occur in the atmosphere. All of this has one thing in common: the movement of fluids. Substances such as water and air are called "fluids". Scientists use the Navier–Stokes equations to explain these movements with mathematics. These equations actually try to tell us the following:

"When a liquid or gas moves, what happens next?" This is where the problem begins.

What's the big question?

The Navier–Stokes equations have been used for years. But there is a very fundamental question that mathematicians do not know the answer to for sure: Do these equations always produce a smooth and controlled solution? Or can the behavior of the fluid become so complex in some special cases that the mathematical solution becomes "infinite"? Let's try to understand this from everyday life. Think of water flowing from a tap. The flow of water can be quite regular at first. However, when vortices, turbulence, and different forces enter the flow, the system can become incredibly complex.

The question mathematicians have been asking for decades is: Will this complexity remain under control forever, or can an "explosion" occur at some point mathematically? This is the question at the heart of the Navier–Stokes problem.

Why is it so important?

Because this is not just a mathematical problem. Navier–Stokes equations;

It is one of the basic tools in our understanding of air movements, ocean currents, movement of liquids, atmospheric phenomena, engineering systems and aerodynamics.

In other words: We are trying to understand a very wide world, from the air movements around an airplane to the currents in the oceans. For this reason, the Clay Mathematics Institute has determined the Navier-Stokes problem as one of the most important mathematical questions in the world. The problem is among the 7 Millennium Problems announced in 2000. For each of these problems, a $1 million reward has been set for researchers who find solutions.

So what did artificial intelligence do?

According to OpenAI's statement on September 8, 2026, an experimental artificial intelligence system developed by the company has created the solution to the Navier–Stokes problem.

OpenAI states that the system is also more powerful than the existing GPT-6 Astra model. What is even more remarkable is the calculation method used. According to the company's statement, the system ran for about 88 hours and used up to 10,000 artificial intelligence agents simultaneously. So we're not talking about a single chatbot answering in a few seconds. A large number of artificial intelligence systems working together with each other are used in a huge mathematical research process.

Did artificial intelligence really solve the problem?

This is the most important point. It is too early to say "definitively solved" yet. OpenAI has published the solution and the formal verification of the solution created with a computer programming language called Lean.

But there is a very important rule in mathematics: Just because an artificial intelligence says it is correct does not mean that it is mathematically correct. The solution needs to be examined in detail by independent mathematicians. Other researchers also need to verify that there is no error in the solution. Therefore, the most accurate statement for today is: "OpenAI has announced that it has solved the Navier-Stokes problem." The next task of the scientific world will be to examine this solution.

The real revolution here may be bigger than mathematics

Perhaps the most important aspect of this development is that artificial intelligence is no longer just a tool that answers questions. In the past, when you said to an artificial intelligence: "Solve this problem", it was expected to produce an answer from the available information. Now, artificial intelligence systems;

It can generate ideas, try different solutions, eliminate erroneous approaches, search for new mathematical connections and control the results with computer-aided systems.

Therefore, we may be at the beginning of a very important change in the world of mathematics.

Does this mean that mathematicians are now redundant?

Absolutely not. Quite the contrary. Artificial intelligence can produce a new solution. But one still needs to ask the following questions:

Is this solution really correct? Why is it correct? What other problems can it be used for? Does this method teach us new mathematical knowledge? Can the method found by artificial intelligence be understood by humans?

These are still very important tasks of human researchers.

Perhaps the mathematician of the future will not be the person who does all the calculations himself; he will be the person who can choose the right one among the millions of ideas produced by artificial intelligence.

How will mathematics change in the future?

20 years ago, a math student might have had to work for hours, days, or even years to solve a problem. In the future, a student will be able to say to artificial intelligence: "Research previously untested solution methods for this problem." Artificial intelligence will be able to try hundreds of approaches. Humans will examine the most promising ones. This situation is not only mathematics; It could also change physics, astronomy, engineering, biology and space exploration.

Because an important part of the great advances in science is being able to apply the right method to the right question. Perhaps the best part of this news is here. As artificial intelligence starts to solve mathematical problems, the meaning of learning mathematics does not disappear. On the contrary, it becomes even more important. Because the scientists of the future will use artificial intelligence not only to get answers; to ask better questions. A 10-year-old child of today may say to artificial intelligence in the future: "Can you find a solution that no one has thought of before?" instead of saying: "Tell me the answer." This is where the real change begins.

Human + Artificial Intelligence = New Science

Age

The Navier-Stokes problem reminds us of something important: The boundaries of science can always be pushed a little further. Yesterday, only humans were doing this. Today, humans and artificial intelligence work together. Tomorrow, perhaps an idea put forward by artificial intelligence will be developed by a human and will be the beginning of a new branch of science. We don't know how far this will go yet. But now we can ask a much more important question: Can artificial intelligence solve a mathematical problem? Maybe this is no longer the real question. How many problems can humanity solve with artificial intelligence that could not be solved before?

To see the future, sometimes it is enough to look at the right question, not the telescope.
