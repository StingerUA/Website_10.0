A New Frontier, New Challenges: Emerging Challenges in Space

Emerging Challenges in Space

by Precious B. Adeleye

New Horizons of Space

Space opens up new horizons for humanity. In 2026 alone, the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope was launched to investigate some of the universe's greatest mysteries and better observe the depths of space, while astronauts made a journey around the Moon on the Artemis II mission. Space activities; From advancing scientific knowledge to improving communication, navigation and our understanding of the Earth and the universe, it has brought tremendous benefits to humanity. But with each new frontier comes new challenges.

This brings us to the challenges that arise in space.

Let's address the issue of sustainability.

Sustainability and Environmental Impact

Every space launch carried out releases rocket exhaust and other emissions into the atmosphere. As the number of launches continues to rise, so do concerns about the environmental impact of space activities. Beyond emissions, the production of spacecraft and launch systems also requires significant amounts of energy and resources. So, the big question is: how can we continue to expand our activities in space while preventing the environmental cost from becoming unsustainable?

Space Debris and Orbital Density

There is also the issue of space debris and orbital density. As the number of countries and private companies with the capacity to explore and operate in space increases, the number of objects placed in orbit continues to increase. Satellites that have lost their function, rocket stages that have completed their mission, and parts formed as a result of collisions can remain in orbit and move at extremely high speeds, posing a risk to active spacecraft.

A collision could lead to the formation of more debris, increasing the likelihood of new collisions and making it increasingly difficult to conduct safe operations in some orbital regions. This raises an important question: As we continue to expand our activities in space, what orbital environment do we leave for future generations?

Artificial Intelligence and Autonomous Systems

Finally, there is an increasing use of artificial intelligence and autonomous systems in space. Artificial intelligence, machine learning and automation; It is transforming the way spacecraft operate, process information and make decisions with limited human intervention. However, this situation; It also raises important questions about human control, responsibility and accountability.

What happens if an autonomous system makes the wrong decision? Who is held accountable when things go wrong? Safety concerns also arise, especially when an AI-powered decision could put human lives, spacecraft, and even celestial bodies at risk. As we grant more autonomy to machines in space, we must also consider the extent to which decision-making should be left to them and where human responsibility should remain.

The Future of Space and Responsibility

The future of space is not just about how far we can go or what we can achieve; it's also about how responsibly we use and protect this new frontier.

The future is not about dominating space, but about our responsibility towards space.
