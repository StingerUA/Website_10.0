ExoMars

Spacemates,

I am Albamen from Albaris, traveling with curiosity and my daughter Lara, and today I'm excited to tell you about ExoMars, a patient hunt for traces of life on Mars. 🔴🚀

ExoMars isn't just one rover. It's a program built by the European Space Agency and Roscosmos to explore Mars from orbit and from the ground.

Why Mars? Long ago it was warmer and wetter; rivers and lakes may have existed and conditions could have been right for life.

Finding those ancient signs isn't easy. Sunlight and cosmic rays change the surface. So scientists decided to look below the crust where secrets can be better preserved.

The program's watchful eye in orbit, the Trace Gas Orbiter, arrived in 2016 and studies Mars' atmosphere, especially methane. Methane can come from living things, but on Mars the source must be carefully investigated.

The planned rover, named Rosalind Franklin, carries a special drill that can take samples down to two meters and a suite of instruments that act like tiny laboratories.

Samples taken from underground are analyzed for chemical traces and microscopic patterns. A speck of rock can be a time capsule with big answers.

ExoMars needs patient teamwork. Rockets and robots are tested again and again because once they travel millions of kilometers, fixing them is nearly impossible.

I can open wormholes in a blink, but human explorers use patience, testing, and cooperation to reach the stars—and that is a superpower too.

This mission helps us understand how planets change and whether life can begin in places very different from Earth.

Imagine reading a hidden page from a very old book buried in sand; the rover is the careful reader, and scientists are the team turning pages. 🔍📖

Keep wondering, ask brave questions, and learn together. Perhaps one day you will design the next tool that uncovers a cosmic secret.
