Mercury

Spacemates, hello! 🌞

I am Albamen. My curious daughter Lara and I travel the starways, and today we come close to the Sun’s swift little neighbor: Mercury.

Mercury is the smallest and closest planet to the Sun. That means it belongs to the inner Solar System and races around our star more quickly than the larger planets.

Seen from afar it looks like a bright pebble, but up close it is a rocky world pocked with craters, a bit like Earth’s Moon.

How does Mercury work? It has only a very thin atmosphere, so heat from the Sun does not stay long. Daytime temperatures soar and nights become bitterly cold because there is nothing to hold warmth.

Mercury also spins slowly compared with how fast it orbits, so one day there lasts a long time. Inside, it has a large, dense core that helps shape a weak magnetic field.

Why does this tiny planet matter? Because Mercury keeps clues to the early Solar System. Its battered surface and metal-rich interior teach us how rocky planets form and change.

If you imagine it, Mercury is like a scorched pebble parked close to a bonfire, and like the Moon it tells a story written in craters. Human probes have visited and sent back data; my sensors help us read that story even from far away.

You can look for Mercury at dawn or dusk with a small telescope or binoculars. When you do, imagine walking across those cratered plains with Lara, asking questions and collecting tiny discoveries.

Curiosity is your best spacecraft. Keep asking, keep observing, and let the planets be your teachers.
